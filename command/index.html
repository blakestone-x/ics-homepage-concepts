  1. T+00:00 · 7:04p
    P1 · COLD-CHAINC-STORE

    Blowing air, not cooling

    Evening call at a Dallas c-store. The walk-in fans are turning but the box is climbing and the product is on the clock. Our tech starts where every ICS call starts — define the concern before touching anything.

  2. T+00:18 · 7:22p
    DIAGNOSIS

    The compressor is done

    Readings confirm it. This is not a repair, it is a replacement. He calls dispatch with the model, the readings and photos before anyone talks about money.

  3. T+00:37 · 7:41p
    SOURCINGNTE CONFIRMED

    Dispatch finds the unit in Fort Worth

    Dispatch works the supply network while the tech stays on the line, and a matching compressor turns up in Fort Worth. Scope and not-to-exceed are agreed against your account before the truck moves — no surprise at month end.

  4. T+02:31 · 9:35p
    ROUND TRIP

    There and back in the same truck

    Fort Worth and back, in the truck already carrying the rest of the job. This is the part a broker chain cannot compress: nobody schedules a second visit for next week, and nobody waits on a sub to accept the ticket in the morning.

  5. T+04:44 · 11:48p
    RUNNING

    Off the roof, box pulling down

    New compressor set, brazed, evacuated, charged and started. He watches the box pull down before he packs up. The same day it failed, the store is cold again.

  6. T+04:55 · 11:59p
    CLOSEDPHOTOS ATTACHED

    Documented before he leaves the lot

    Readings, photos and the replaced part logged against the asset and closed out in whatever platform your program runs on — so the next person to open this site’s history sees exactly what happened and why.

Illustrative scenario · Every ICS call runs the same 4-step process: define & quantify → co-author the plan → execute on schedule → prove the results.
